bootsplash

Draw a boot splash for the Osborne 1, and export it as the animation the RP2354B plays. The screen is 128×32 cells; the viewport is 52, 80 or 104 columns × 24 rows.
Click a cell, then type — every printable character is its own glyph, capitals included. Arrows move, Enter is the next row, Space is the blank, drag to select a block, Tab flips bright/dim, and Ctrl/Cmd-C, X, V are copy, cut and paste. The 32 block glyphs and the hatch come from the palette.

Drag a thumbnail to re-order the animation — the strip is the play order. Copy to position plants a copy anywhere: with twelve frames, frame 11 copied to 13 makes the copy frame 13, and anything that was there moves along.
The offset moves the picture: columns +4 is four cells right, rows −2 is two up. The rectangle is cleared first, and what moves out of it is clipped, not wrapped.

Selection & clipboard

Drag on the grid to make a rectangle; with nothing selected these act on the single cell under the cursor. The clipboard keeps both planes — the glyph and the ninth bit — so it does not go through art text.

Pen

Glyph palette

The palette shows only what the pen mode can place: the 95 standard characters (0x20–0x7E) or the 34 graphical blocks (0x00–0x1F, the blank, and the 0x7F hatch). All 128 glyphs are available in any cell — the chargen is indexed with chr & 0x7f — one mode switch away. Every swatch is labelled with the key that reaches it, so the palette is also the key map. Clicking a swatch makes it the pen; clicking it again clears it.

Export

The .bin is a 32-byte header and then the frames, each byte-identical to firmware/rom-service/frame.bin — tail -c +33 of a one-frame file is that asset exactly. The header's flags byte carries the playback mode (bit 0 loop, bit 1 ping-pong). The header is this tool's proposal; the firmware side of #247 is not written yet.
.txt is the repo's art text, which the ROM's own loader reads with sui 65 — so it cannot carry a cell holding glyph 0x41–0x61, and with direct keys a capital is exactly one of those. The export counts them and says so; the .bin is unaffected, because a frame is glyph codes and has no such limit.

Import

A .bin in this tool's container (or a bare 8192-byte frame), or art text in the repo's own convention. Art text cannot express a cell holding glyph 0x41–0x61; if one arrives it is reported rather than silently changed.
